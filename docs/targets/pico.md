# Target: Raspberry Pi Pico W with a Pico Display Pack 2.0

The meter's first target. Build it with `make pico`; the
[README](../../README.md) covers the build and what the meter shows, and
[USAGE.md](../USAGE.md) how to use it.

## Hardware

- Raspberry Pi Pico W (RP2040 + Infineon CYW43439 Wi-Fi).
- [Pimoroni Pico Display Pack 2.0](https://shop.pimoroni.com/products/pico-display-pack-2-0)
  plugged onto the Pico's headers: a 320x240 IPS panel (ST7789V), four
  buttons and an RGB LED.

| Display Pack function | Pico W GPIO |
|---|---|
| LCD CS / SCLK / MOSI (SPI0) | 17 / 18 / 19 |
| LCD DC | 16 |
| Backlight | 20 |
| Buttons A / B / X / Y | 12 / 13 / 14 / 15 |
| LED red / green / blue | 6 / 7 / 8 |

None of these clash with the CYW43439's pins (23, 24, 25, 29). The
devicetree overlay and the board's settings are
`app/boards/rpi_pico_rp2040_w.overlay` and `.conf`. If the picture is
upside down on your unit, change the panel's `mdac` there from `0x60` to
`0xa0`.

The buttons are beside the screen, A and B on the left and X and Y on the
right; the RGB LED is to the right of the screen.

## Build

```sh
make pico                                # out/pico/network-meter.uf2
make pico WIFI_COUNTRY=UNITED_STATES     # another regulatory domain
```

`WIFI_COUNTRY` is a country name from Infineon's WHD
(`whd_country_code_t` in `whd_types.h`) without its `WHD_COUNTRY_`
prefix. The default is `UNITED_KINGDOM`, which allows channels 1–13; the
US stops at 11. The firmware is built with Arm Toolchain for Embedded
(clang).

## Flashing

1. Hold the Pico W's **BOOTSEL** button while plugging it into USB. It
   appears as a drive called `RPI-RP2`.
2. Copy `out/pico/network-meter.uf2` to that drive. The Pico restarts into
   the meter.

For SWD (a Raspberry Pi Debug Probe), `out/pico/network-meter.elf` works
with OpenOCD or probe-rs. `west flash` also works from a host with a west
workspace.

A UF2 update writes only the firmware's own blocks, so the event log, in
the last 64 KB of flash (`storage_partition`), survives it.

## Serial console

The console is a USB serial port (CDC-ACM) on the Pico's own USB socket:
`/dev/cu.usbmodem<N>` on a Mac (`ls /dev/cu.usbmodem*` finds it),
`/dev/ttyACM<N>` on Linux. Open it with, for example,
`screen /dev/cu.usbmodem1101 115200` (any baud rate works; quit with
Ctrl-A then K). Logging starts when the port is opened, so the first
moments after power-up are not shown. While nothing has the port open,
the meter doesn't wait for it (`app/src/target/pico/usb_console.c`).

## Battery and case

[hardware/pico/](../../hardware/pico/README.md) has a 3D-printable case,
generated by a Python script, with a Pimoroni LiPo Amigo Pro charger and a
350 mAh LiPo behind the Pico, the [wiring diagram](../../hardware/pico/wiring.svg)
and the [bill of materials](../../hardware/pico/BOM.md). The charger's
USB-C socket charges the battery. To flash or use the console, open the
case: the Pico's own USB socket is inside.

## Known issues and limits

- **2.4 GHz only.** The CYW43439 can't hear 5 GHz networks.
- **The AIROC driver leaks.** After about 12 hours of scans, the AIROC
  Wi-Fi driver ran out of packet buffers for good (the console showed
  `failed to allocate a packet buffer for IOVAR` and `Failed to start scan`
  every 2 seconds). They leak somewhere in the driver or its WHD library,
  and only a restart gets them back. So after 5 failed scans in a row
  (`CONFIG_METER_RECOVER_AFTER`), the meter restarts itself, which takes a
  few seconds, and logs a "Wi-Fi restart".
- **Hidden networks** need a carried Zephyr patch
  (`airoc-hidden-network-results.patch`): without it the driver reports
  their channel and signal as zero.
- **Unexplained restarts can't be told apart.** The reset button, a
  debugger and the hardware watchdog all show as "restart after …": the
  RP2040's reset register keeps the last power-on until the next one, so
  the meter doesn't record a hardware reset cause on this board.
- **The Display Pack sits over the antenna** (the end opposite the USB
  socket), which costs a little range.
- **Hardware watchdog**: 4 s, within the RP2040's 8.3 s limit.

## Things to check on new hardware

- Panel orientation and colours (`mdac`, `inversion-off` in the overlay).
- The 62.5 MHz SPI clock to the panel (`mipi-max-frequency`). Lower it if
  the picture is garbled.
- Scan times. The scan timeout is `CONFIG_METER_SCAN_TIMEOUT_S` (default
  15 s); a scan that runs over it is shown with what it heard.
